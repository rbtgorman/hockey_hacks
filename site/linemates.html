Side question

Do linemates score together?

When two teammates spend most of their ice time together, do they both end up on the scoresheet more often? Yes, and the more time they share, the stronger it gets. Here's what I found in four seasons of games, in plain words.

How to read the numbers

Time together
How much of their ice time two teammates spend out there at the same moment. Out of all the time at least one of them is on the ice, how much of it are they both on? 0% means they're never out together. Regular linemates who change as a unit are above 55%.
Times as often as chance
Say one player gets a point in half his games and his linemate gets one in 40% of theirs. If the two had nothing to do with each other, they'd both get a point in 50% × 40% = 20% of games, just by chance. If they really both get one in 30% of games, that's 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5 times as often as chance. 1× means no connection at all. Each pair is compared with its own scoring, so a top line can't look connected just because it scores a lot.
Point
A goal or either assist. Shootout goals don't count.
Even strength, power play
Even strength here means 5-on-5 with both goalies in. Power play means one side has more skaters, with both goalies in. Time together and points are counted separately for each.

The more they play together, the more their points come together

Each dot is a group of teammate pairs, sorted by how much of their ice time they share. 1× is chance. The thin bar through each dot shows the range the number could reasonably be in.

What it found

  1. Teammates who are rarely out together aren't connected. At even strength, pairs who share less than 10% of their ice time both get a point 1.05 times as often as chance. That's basically chance.
  2. It grows step by step. At even strength it goes 1.26×, 1.38×, 1.47×, then 2.14× for regular linemates, as the time together goes up.
  3. The power play is the extreme. Teammates on the same unit both get a power-play point 2.75 times as often as chance. Teammates on different units come in under chance, at 0.73×. When one unit's on the ice the other is on the bench, so they're splitting the same minutes.
  4. It's the same goals. The share of both-point games where they were in on the same goal climbs from 10% to 88% at even strength, and 90% on the power play. It isn't "the whole team had a big night." One of them is setting up the other.
  5. Forwards with forwards and forwards with defensemen look about the same. Defense partners only show it once they're a regular pair: 1.84× at even strength when they're together more than 55% of the time.
  6. Stars hit a ceiling. MacKinnon and Rantanen in 2023-24 come out at only 1.13×. MacKinnon got a point in 84% of his games, which leaves almost no room to beat chance: the most this pair could possibly show is 1.19×. For pairs like that, the better number is how often their points came on the same goal, which was 85%.

How I checked it

  • Season points match the NHL's official totals for five stars I checked by hand. McDavid 2022-23 comes out at 64 goals and 89 assists, 153 points, same as the record book.
  • No pair is ever counted as together for more time than either of them actually played.
  • The ranges are tight. For regular linemates at even strength it's 2.11× to 2.18×, so this isn't a fluke of which games happened to be in the data.
  • Pairs who only played a few games together can blur the numbers, so I reran it with only pairs who played 20 or more games together. Same answer: 2.13× instead of 2.14×.
  • This season so far (330 games) shows the same pattern: 2.04× for regular linemates at even strength. The power play is a bit lower so far (2.23×), maybe because teams shuffle their units early in the year.

Why it matters for this site

If this site ever predicts player points, it can't predict each player on his own. It has to simulate the goals first, then hand out the goal and the assists to the players who were on the ice. That's what ties linemates' points together, and it's now written into the plan for the game simulator.

The code is diagnostics/check_linemate_points.py, every table is in results/checks/linemate_points.txt, and the full write-up is in the findings.
