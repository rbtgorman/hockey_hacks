How the numbers work

Every number on this site, broken down the same way: what it is, where it's from, how it's figured, and what it actually tells you. I calculate all of it myself from the NHL's public data, and the code's on GitHub.

The data

Games and shots

What it is
The games and shots everything else is built on.
Where it's from
The NHL's public API (api-web.nhle.com and api.nhle.com): play-by-play, shift charts and box scores. It all goes into a PostgreSQL database (the code's in ingest/). The counts come from results/v1_2_fenwick_train.log and site/data/periods_index.json.
How it's figured
Only finished games get loaded, so a game that's still going never counts. 5,794 games (regular season and playoffs, October 7, 2022 to October 28, 2026) with 499,443 unblocked shots. The period page only uses the regular season: 1,312 games in each full season and 202 so far in 2026-27.
What it tells you
Tons of shots, but not a lot of games per team, just 82 a season. That's the main reason this site sizes teams up instead of predicting them.

Unblocked shots

What it is
Every shot that didn't get blocked: goals, saves and missed shots. Analytics people call this Fenwick.
Where it's from
features/build_features_fenwick.py, which builds the shot_features_fenwick table.
How it's figured
Blocked shots and shootout attempts are out. So are two things the NHL only started logging partway through: shots blocked by your own teammate (from 2023-24) and failed bank attempts (from 2024-25).
What it tells you
Saves and misses get counted together, so if the league changes how it splits them up, the total stays the same. And it did change: missed shots went from 28.3% of attempts in 2022-23 to 34.5% in 2025-26, while goals stayed around 7% (the event mix in the training log).

Shot quality (xG)

Expected goals (xG) on a shot

On the site: xG model, Period scoring

What it is
How likely a shot was to go in, from 0 to 1. An xG of 0.10 is a 1-in-10 chance.
Where it's from
Model v1.2-fenwick (model/train_v1_2_fenwick.py). Every shot's xG gets saved in the shot_xg table by model/score_fenwick.py.
How it's figured
A machine-learning model (LightGBM, 167 decision trees) went through 364,074 shots from 2022-23 to 2024-25 and learned which ones end up in the net. For each shot it looks at 17 things: how far out and what angle; where on the ice it came from; the type of shot; 5-on-5, power play and so on; whether the net was empty; the score; the period and how much time is left; what happened right before (what it was, which team did it, and how many seconds and feet ago); and whether it was a rebound, a rebound off the post, or off the rush. Distance, empty net and angle matter most (results/v1_2-fenwick/feature_importance.csv).
What it tells you
Out of a bunch of shots just like this one, how many you'd expect to go in. It's about the chance, not the player: the model doesn't know who shot it or who was in net. Add up xG over a bunch of shots and you get expected goals for a team, a period or a season.

How often shots go in

On the site: xG model

What it is
How many unblocked shots turned into goals.
Where it's from
results/v1_2-fenwick/metrics.json, test season.
How it's figured
Goals ÷ unblocked shots. 2025-26: 8,572 ÷ 118,605 = 7.2%, about 1 in 14.
What it tells you
That's your average shot. Every xG number is measured against it.

Is the model any good?

All three checks use the 2025-26 season: 118,605 shots the model never saw while I was building it. Testing on games it's never seen is the only honest way to know if it works.

Goals vs. what it predicted (O/E)

On the site: xG model

What it is
Real goals ÷ the goals the model called for. O/E is short for observed over expected.
Where it's from
results/v1_2-fenwick/metrics.json, test season.
How it's figured
Add up the xG on every 2025-26 shot to get predicted goals, then divide the real goals by that. 8,572 goals ÷ 8,745 predicted = 0.98.
What it tells you
1.00 means it's right on average. 0.98 means it called for about 2% too many goals. My older models, which only learned from shots on goal, were at 1.07 to 1.08 on 2024-25 (results/leaderboard.md).

Good chance vs. bad (AUC)

On the site: xG model

What it is
How well the model sorts shots. Does a goal get a higher xG than a shot that didn't go in?
Where it's from
results/v1_2-fenwick/metrics.json, test season.
How it's figured
Take every pair of one goal and one non-goal from 2025-26. AUC is how often the goal had the higher xG (a tie counts as half). 0.779.
What it tells you
0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 would be perfect. It doesn't tell you if the percentages themselves are right. That's what O/E and the next check are for.

Biggest miss

On the site: xG model, with the chart

What it is
Whether you can take the model's percentages at face value all the way up and down, not just on average.
Where it's from
results/v1_2-fenwick/reliability.csv.
How it's figured
Sort the 2025-26 shots by xG and split them into ten even groups of about 11,860. In each group, compare the average xG with how many actually went in. The biggest gap is in the fifth group: 4.30% predicted, 3.93% went in, 0.38 percentage points apart.
What it tells you
If every group is close, a 5% shot and a 25% shot are both priced right. A game simulator adds these chances up, so getting them right matters more than ranking shots perfectly.

Goals for every 100 expected

On the site: xG model, under "Where it still struggles"

What it is
O/E for one kind of shot, times 100.
Where it's from
results/v1_2-fenwick/segments.csv, the oe_v1.2 column.
How it's figured
Goals ÷ total xG for that kind of shot in 2025-26, × 100. Tip-ins 78, shots from 60+ feet 88, power play 94.
What it tells you
100 means it's right. Under 100, the model's too high on that kind of shot. That's where the next version needs to get better.

Period scoring

The period page pulls from site/data/periods_<season>.json (one row per team per regular-season game) and site/data/periods_index.json (each stat's league norm and a couple of constants). export/period_tables.py builds both from the database. Your browser does the rest of the math, so every number updates when you switch the season, the window or the stat.

GP and Hits

What it is
GP is games played in whatever window you picked (whole season or last 10). Hits is how many of those games it actually happened.
Where it's from
The y_ columns in periods_<season>.json: a 1 or a 0 for each game and stat.
How it's figured
Every game gets a yes or no for each of the 15 stats, based on the goals in periods 1 through 3. A 2–1 first period counts for "1st period: 2+ goals" and "1st period: both teams score," but not for "1st period ends 0-0."
What it tells you
The raw record. By itself it doesn't tell you anything about luck.

Rate

What it is
How often it happened.
Where it's from
Figured right on the page.
How it's figured
Hits ÷ GP. 6 hits in 10 games is 60%.
What it tells you
What happened, not what's going to happen. Over 10 games, one more hit moves it 10 points.

League norm

What it is
How often it happens in a normal NHL game.
Where it's from
baseline for each stat in periods_index.json.
How it's figured
How often it happened across all 5,248 regular-season games from 2022-23 through 2025-26. "1st period: 2+ goals": 53.8% (54.7%, 54.1%, 52.5% and 53.7% by season).
What it tells you
The measuring stick every team gets compared to. It's four full seasons, so it doesn't move during this one.

Range

What it is
Where the team's real rate could be, based on just the games so far.
Where it's from
Figured right on the page (wilson() in periods.html).
How it's figured
It's called a Wilson interval, set to 95%. With p = Rate, n = GP and z = 1.96: the middle is (p + z²/2n) ÷ (1 + z²/n), and it stretches z × √(p(1 − p)/n + z²/4n²) ÷ (1 + z²/n) each way. 6 hits in 10 games gives 31% to 83%.
What it tells you
If the range covers the league norm, you can't rule out luck. Wide ranges are normal early in the season. Even if all 32 teams were exactly average, about 2 would land outside their range just by chance.

Shrunk

What it is
My best guess at the team's real rate.
Where it's from
k for each stat in periods_index.json, from regression_k() in export/period_math.py.
How it's figured
(Hits + k × norm) ÷ (GP + k). The k comes from Tom Tango's regression to the mean, a method out of baseball stats. Take four seasons of team records and see how spread out the teams are. Take away the spread luck alone would cause, and what's left is the real difference between teams. Then k = norm × (1 − norm) ÷ that real spread. "1st period: 2+ goals" has k = 356. A team that's 6 for 10: (6 + 356 × 0.5375) ÷ (10 + 356) = 53.9%.
What it tells you
k is how many games it takes before a team's own record counts as much as the league norm. When luck explains all of the spread, there's no k, and every team's Shrunk is just the norm.

Exp. (xG) and "What the shots say"

What it is
How many games it should've happened in, going by the shots both teams actually took.
Where it's from
The e_ columns in periods_<season>.json, from expected() in export/period_tables.py. The adjustment is xg_factor in periods_index.json.
How it's figured
In each game, every unblocked shot in the period counts as its own chance at its xG. That gives you the odds of 0 goals, 1 goal, 2 goals and so on (a Poisson-binomial distribution, if you want the name), and from there the odds it happened. Two shots at 0.30 xG each: no goals 49%, 2+ goals 9%. Add those odds up over the team's games. Treating every shot as its own chance runs a little low on "2+ goals" and high on scoreless periods, so each stat gets adjusted by how often it really happened compared with the estimate over 2022-26: × 1.0085 for "1st period: 2+ goals."
What it tells you
What the team's games deserved based on the shots alone. Ties don't get an xG number, because treating shots as separate chances misses on ties by about 7 points.

vs xG

What it is
Hits minus Exp. (xG).
Where it's from
Figured right on the page.
How it's figured
7 hits against 5.2 expected is +1.8.
What it tells you
A plus means it happened more than the shots deserved, from finishing, goaltending or plain luck. A minus means less. It can't tell you which of those three it was.

Goals and xG by period

What it is
Goals for and against in each period, with what the shots were worth (xG) in small type underneath.
Where it's from
The gf, ga, xgf and xga columns in periods_<season>.json.
How it's figured
Goals come straight from the play-by-play. xG adds up the xG of every unblocked shot in that period. Regulation is periods 1 through 3 together.
What it tells you
More goals than xG means the finishing or the goaltending beat the shots, or the bounces did. Over a handful of games, it's usually the bounces.

Colors and arrows

What it is
▲ Orange: more often (or more goals) than you'd expect. ▼ Blue: less often (or fewer). No color: could just be luck.
Where it's from
Figured right on the page (tone() in periods.html).
How it's figured
A cell only gets colored when luck alone would do that less than about 1 time in 20. Rate: when the range doesn't cover the league norm. vs xG: when (Hits − Exp.) ÷ √(sum of q × (1 − q)) is past ±1.96, where q is each game's odds. Goals: when (goals − xG) ÷ √xG is past ±1.96.
What it tells you
A colored cell is worth a closer look, but it's not proof. With 32 teams and 15 stats, some cells are going to light up by chance.

Ground rules for every stat

Regulation only
Periods 1 through 3. OT and shootout goals don't count.
Empty-net goals
They count. One stat takes them out of the 3rd, where they're about 17% of goals (2022-26).
First 10 minutes
A goal by either team before 10:00 of the 1st.
Last 10
Each team's last ten games.
Regular season
Playoff games aren't on the period page.

What this data can't tell you yet

To predict a game, you need to know how good each team really is. For these period stats, one season of games isn't enough to find out. The table shows k for each stat: how many games it takes before a team's own record counts as much as the league norm.

Seasons' worth is k ÷ 82. "No real difference" means four seasons of records didn't spread out any more than luck would, so every team's best guess is the norm. From site/data/periods_index.json.

  • The model doesn't know players yet. Shooters and goalies aren't in it, so a top-line sniper and a fourth-line grinder get the same xG for the same shot.
  • The way shots get recorded looks like it changed partway through, with more saves getting written down as misses, so older seasons don't line up perfectly with newer ones.
  • The tracking data's only public as summaries. The NHL puts out the play-by-play, but its player-tracking data (NHL EDGE) only comes out as totals.

Before this site predicts games, it needs shooters and goalies in the model, more seasons under the current way of tracking shots, and a game simulator that's been tested on a season it never saw.
