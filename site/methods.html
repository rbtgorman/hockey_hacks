How the numbers work

Every number on this site, in the same four parts: what it is, where it comes from, how it's calculated, and what it means. All of them are computed by this project from the NHL's public data, and the code is on GitHub.

The data

Games and shot attempts

What it is
The games and shot attempts every other number is built from.
Where it comes from
The NHL's public API (api-web.nhle.com and api.nhle.com): play-by-play, shift charts and boxscores, stored in a PostgreSQL database by the code in ingest/. Counts from results/v1_2_fenwick_train.log and site/data/periods_index.json.
How it's counted
Only finished games are loaded, so a game in progress never counts. 5,794 games (regular season and playoffs, October 7, 2022 to October 28, 2026) with 499,443 unblocked shot attempts. The period page uses regular-season games only: 1,312 in each full season and 202 so far in 2026-27.
What it means
Lots of shots, but not many games per team: 82 a season. That gap is the main reason this site describes teams instead of projecting them.

Unblocked shot attempts

What it is
Every shot attempt that wasn't blocked: goals, shots on goal (saves) and missed shots. Hockey analysts call this Fenwick.
Where it comes from
features/build_features_fenwick.py, which builds the shot_features_fenwick table.
How it's counted
Blocked shots and shootout attempts are left out. So are two event types the NHL logs in only part of the window: teammate blocks (from 2023-24) and failed bank attempts (from 2024-25).
What it means
Saves and misses are counted together, so a change in how the league splits them doesn't change the total. That change happened: missed shots went from 28.3% of attempts in 2022-23 to 34.5% in 2025-26, while goals stayed at about 7% (event mix in the training log).

Shot quality (xG)

Expected goals (xG) of a shot

Shown on: xG model, Period scoring

What it is
The chance that an unblocked attempt becomes a goal, from 0 to 1. An xG of 0.10 is a 1-in-10 chance.
Where it comes from
Model v1.2-fenwick (model/train_v1_2_fenwick.py). Each attempt's xG is stored in the shot_xg table by model/score_fenwick.py.
How it's calculated
A machine-learning model (LightGBM, 167 decision trees) learned from 364,074 attempts in 2022-23 to 2024-25 which situations end in goals. For each attempt it looks at 17 facts: distance and angle to the net; where on the ice it came from; shot type; strength (5-on-5, power play and so on); whether the net was empty; the score difference; the period and time left in it; what happened just before (the event, which team did it, how many seconds and feet ago); and whether it was a rebound, a rebound off the post, or on a rush. Distance, empty net and angle matter most (results/v1_2-fenwick/feature_importance.csv).
What it means
Of many attempts like this one, the share that would score. It describes the chance, not the player: the model doesn't know who shot or who was in goal. Adding up xG over many shots gives expected goals for a team, a period or a season.

Goals per attempt

Shown on: xG model

What it is
The share of unblocked attempts that became goals.
Where it comes from
results/v1_2-fenwick/metrics.json, test season.
How it's calculated
Goals ÷ unblocked attempts. 2025-26: 8,572 ÷ 118,605 = 7.2%, about 1 in 14.
What it means
The average shot. Every xG is a chance measured against this.

Checking the model

All three checks use the 2025-26 season: 118,605 attempts the model never saw while it was built. Testing on unseen games is the only honest way to know whether it works.

Goals ÷ predicted (O/E)

Shown on: xG model

What it is
Goals actually scored ÷ goals the model predicted. O/E stands for observed over expected.
Where it comes from
results/v1_2-fenwick/metrics.json, test split.
How it's calculated
Add up the xG of every 2025-26 attempt to get predicted goals, then divide actual goals by it. 8,572 goals ÷ 8,745 predicted = 0.98.
What it means
1.00 is right on average. 0.98 means the model expected about 2% too many goals. The earlier models that learned from shots on goal only were at 1.07 to 1.08 on 2024-25 (results/leaderboard.md).

Ranking (AUC)

Shown on: xG model

What it is
How well the model sorts shots: does a goal get a higher xG than a shot that didn't go in?
Where it comes from
results/v1_2-fenwick/metrics.json, test split.
How it's calculated
Take every pair of one goal and one non-goal from 2025-26. AUC is the share of pairs where the goal had the higher xG (a tie counts half). 0.779.
What it means
0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 would be perfect. AUC says nothing about whether the percentages themselves are right; O/E and calibration check that.

Largest calibration gap

Shown on: xG model, with the chart

What it is
Whether the model's percentages can be taken at face value across the whole range, not just on average.
Where it comes from
results/v1_2-fenwick/reliability.csv.
How it's calculated
Sort the 2025-26 attempts by xG and cut them into ten equal groups of about 11,860. In each group, compare the average xG with the share that scored. The largest gap is in the fifth group: 4.30% predicted, 3.93% scored, a gap of 0.38 percentage points.
What it means
A small gap in every group means a 5% shot and a 25% shot are both priced right. A game simulator adds these chances up, so it needs them right more than it needs a perfect ranking.

Goals for every 100 predicted

Shown on: xG model, under "Where it's still weak"

What it is
O/E for one group of shots, times 100.
Where it comes from
results/v1_2-fenwick/segments.csv, column oe_v1.2.
How it's calculated
Goals ÷ summed xG for that group in 2025-26, × 100. Tip-ins 78, shots from 60+ feet 88, power play 94.
What it means
100 is right. Below 100, the model over-rates that kind of shot. These are where the next version should improve.

Period scoring

The period page reads site/data/periods_<season>.json, one row per team per regular-season game, and site/data/periods_index.json, which holds each measure's league norm and constants. export/period_tables.py builds both from the database; the page does the rest of the math in your browser, so every number updates when you change the season, window or measure.

GP and Hits

What it is
GP: games played in the window you picked (whole season or last 10). Hits: how many of those games the outcome happened in.
Where it comes from
The y_ columns in periods_<season>.json: a 1 or 0 for each game and measure.
How it's counted
Each game gets a yes or no for each of the 15 measures, from the goals in periods 1–3. A 2–1 first period is a hit for "1st period: 2+ goals" and "1st period: both teams score", and a miss for "1st period ends 0-0".
What it means
The raw record. On its own it says nothing about luck.

Rate

What it is
How often the outcome happened.
Where it comes from
Calculated in the page.
How it's calculated
Hits ÷ GP. 6 hits in 10 games is 60%.
What it means
What happened, not what will happen. Over 10 games, one extra hit moves the rate by 10 points.

League norm

What it is
How often the outcome happens in a typical NHL game.
Where it comes from
baseline for each measure in periods_index.json.
How it's calculated
The share of all 5,248 regular-season games from 2022-23 to 2025-26 where it happened. "1st period: 2+ goals": 53.8% (by season 54.7%, 54.1%, 52.5% and 53.7%).
What it means
The yardstick every team is compared with. It covers four complete seasons, so it doesn't move during the current one.

95% range

What it is
Where the team's true rate could be, given only its games so far.
Where it comes from
Calculated in the page (wilson() in periods.html).
How it's calculated
The Wilson interval, with p = Rate, n = GP and z = 1.96: center = (p + z²/2n) ÷ (1 + z²/n), and half-width = z × √(p(1 − p)/n + z²/4n²) ÷ (1 + z²/n). 6 hits in 10 games gives 31% to 83%.
What it means
If the range covers the league norm, the record could be pure luck. Wide ranges are normal early in a season. Even if all 32 teams were exactly average, about 2 would land outside their range by chance.

Shrunk

What it is
The best single guess of the team's true rate.
Where it comes from
k for each measure in periods_index.json, from regression_k() in export/period_math.py.
How it's calculated
(Hits + k × norm) ÷ (GP + k). k is Tango's regression constant. From four seasons of team records, take how much team rates spread around the league rate, subtract the spread luck alone would cause, and what's left is real team differences. Then k = norm × (1 − norm) ÷ that real spread. "1st period: 2+ goals" has k = 356. A team with 6 hits in 10 games: (6 + 356 × 0.5375) ÷ (10 + 356) = 53.9%.
What it means
k is the number of games at which a team's own record and the league norm count equally. Where luck explains all of the spread, there is no k and every team's Shrunk is the norm.

Exp. (xG) and "What the shots deserved"

What it is
How many games the outcome should have happened in, given the shots both teams actually took.
Where it comes from
The e_ columns in periods_<season>.json, from expected() in export/period_tables.py; the scaling factor is xg_factor in periods_index.json.
How it's calculated
In each game, every unblocked attempt in the period counts as a separate chance at its xG. That gives the probability of each number of goals (a Poisson-binomial distribution), and from it the chance of the outcome. Two shots at 0.30 xG each: no goals 49%, 2+ goals 9%. Add those chances up over the team's games. Treating shots as separate chances runs a little low on "2+ goals" and high on scoreless periods, so each measure is scaled by its 2022-26 ratio of actual to expected: × 1.0085 for "1st period: 2+ goals".
What it means
What the team's games deserved on shot quality alone. Ties get no xG estimate, because treating shots as separate chances misses ties by about 7 points.

vs xG

What it is
Hits minus Exp. (xG).
Where it comes from
Calculated in the page.
How it's calculated
7 hits against 5.2 expected is +1.8.
What it means
Positive: it happened more often than the shots deserved, through finishing, goaltending or luck. Negative: less often. It can't tell those three apart.

Goals and xG by period

What it is
Goals for and against in each period, with expected goals (xG) in small type underneath.
Where it comes from
The gf, ga, xgf and xga columns in periods_<season>.json.
How it's calculated
Goals are counted from the play-by-play. xG adds up the xG of every unblocked attempt in that period. Regulation is periods 1–3 together.
What it means
Goals above xG: finishing or goaltending beat the shots, or luck did. Over a few games, luck is the likeliest reason.

Colors and arrows

What it is
▲ Orange: more often (or more goals) than expected. ▼ Blue: less often (or fewer). No color: within normal luck.
Where it comes from
Calculated in the page (tone() in periods.html).
How it's calculated
A cell is colored only when luck alone would do this less than about 1 time in 20. Rate: when the 95% range misses the league norm. vs xG: when (Hits − Exp.) ÷ √(sum of q × (1 − q)) is beyond ±1.96, where q is each game's chance. Goals: when (goals − xG) ÷ √xG is beyond ±1.96.
What it means
A colored cell is worth a look, not proof. With 32 teams and 15 measures, some cells get colored by chance alone.

Rules for every measure

Regulation only
Periods 1–3. Overtime and shootout goals never count.
Empty-net goals
They count. One measure removes them from the 3rd period, where they are about 17% of goals (2022-26).
First 10 minutes
A goal by either team before 10:00 of the 1st period.
Last 10
Each team's ten most recent games.
Regular season
Playoff games aren't on the period page.

What this data can't tell you yet

Projecting a game needs each team's true level. On these period measures, one season of games isn't enough to find it. The table shows k for each measure: the number of games at which a team's own record counts as much as the league norm.

  • The model doesn't know players yet. Shooter and goalie quality aren't in it, so a star and a fourth-liner get the same xG for the same shot.
  • The way shots are recorded appears to have changed partway through this window, with more saves logged as misses, so older seasons don't line up perfectly with new ones.
  • Tracking data is only public as summaries. The NHL publishes play-by-play events, but its player-tracking data (NHL EDGE) only in aggregated form.

Before this site projects games, it needs shooter and goalie quality in the model, more seasons under the current way of recording shots, and a game simulator that is tested on a season it never saw.
