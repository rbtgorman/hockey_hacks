Hockey Hacks

Groundwork for honest NHL projections. Before this site predicts any games, it builds the measuring tools, tests them on seasons they never saw, and explains what every number means.

Updated October 29, 2026 Model v1.2-fenwick Data: NHL public API How every number is calculated

Why there are no projections yet

Baseball projection sites have a lot to work with: MLB makes pitch-by-pitch tracking data public, and each team plays 162 games. Hockey has less. The NHL publishes play-by-play events, with a location for each shot, but its player-tracking data (NHL EDGE) only as summaries. And with 82 games a season, luck hides a team's true level for a long time.

So this site builds the foundation in order, and each piece is tested before the next one depends on it.

How much luck? For "2+ goals in the 1st period", a team needs about 356 games, more than four seasons, before its own record tells you as much as the league average does. See every measure

The foundation, in order

  1. 1
    Collect the data
    Every NHL game since 2022-23, from the NHL's public API: play-by-play, shifts and boxscores. 5,794 games and 499,443 unblocked shot attempts through October 28, 2026, updated during the season.
    Done
  2. 2
    Measure shot quality
    A model gives every unblocked shot attempt its chance of becoming a goal, called expected goals (xG). It works and is tested below. It doesn't yet know who took the shot or who was in goal.
    In progress
  3. 3
    Describe teams fairly
    Period scoring: how often each team's games hit common period outcomes, next to the range luck alone allows and what the shots deserved.
    In progress
  4. 4
    Simulate games and project them
    Waits on steps 2 and 3: shooter and goalie quality in the model, and more seasons under the NHL's current way of recording shots. A projection gets published only after it's tested on a season it never saw.
    Not started

What it found along the way

The first models learned from shots on goal only, and all of them under-predicted the 2024-25 season by 7–8%. Scoring hadn't really risen: goals per shot on goal went up while goals per unblocked attempt barely moved. That fits some saves now being logged as missed shots, which David Johnson at Hockey Analysis suggested puck tracking allows. Learning from all unblocked attempts (goals, saves and misses) instead cut the miss to 3%. That's why every number on this site uses unblocked attempts.

Where it's still weak

Goals scored for every 100 the model predicted, 2025-26. 100 is right.

  • Tip-ins: 78. The model expects more tip-in goals than happen.
  • Shots from 60+ feet: 88. Long shots go in less often than it predicts.
  • Power play: 94. Shots with a man advantage are slightly over-predicted.
  • Who shot, who saved. The model doesn't know the shooter or the goalie yet, so a star and a fourth-liner get the same xG for the same shot.
